# Title lists: worked examples

Examples of the shape, not lists to copy. Build each from the client's brief.

## Support leader (B2C e-commerce, 50–1,000 employees)

**Titles:** Head of Customer Support, Head of Customer Service, Head of Customer Experience, Head of
CX, Director of Customer Support, Customer Service Director, Customer Experience Director, VP
Customer Support, VP Customer Experience, VP of Customer Service, Chief Customer Officer, Customer
Support Lead, Customer Service Manager, Customer Care Manager, Head of Customer Care

**Description:** Senior people who own the customer support function — the team, its tools and its
response times — in a consumer-facing company. In companies under ~200 employees this is often a
"Customer Service Manager" or "Support Lead" reporting to a founder; above that, a Head, Director
or VP. Excludes agents, specialists, coordinators, team leads of a single shift, and Customer
Success roles that manage B2B accounts rather than support. Best found on LinkedIn; the team page
rarely lists them.

## Finance decision maker (SaaS, 20–500 employees)

**Titles:** CFO, Chief Financial Officer, VP Finance, Head of Finance, Finance Director, Director of
Finance, Financial Controller, Controller, Head of FP&A

**Description:** The person who signs off on finance tooling and owns the finance team's processes.
In companies under ~50 employees there is often no CFO and the Head of Finance or a Controller
decides; above ~300 the CFO delegates tooling to a VP Finance or Controller. Excludes accountants,
analysts, bookkeepers, payroll, AP/AR clerks, and "Finance Business Partner" roles embedded in other
departments.

## Founder / CEO (small companies only)

**Titles:** Founder, Co-Founder, CEO, Chief Executive Officer, Managing Director, Owner, President

**Description:** The top executive of a company small enough (under ~50 people) that they still own
the problem directly. Use as a lower rung capped at one per company; in larger companies they
delegate and a function head is the right person. "Managing Director" is the UK/DACH form of CEO;
"President" in the US may be the CEO or a division head — check the company's size.

## Pitfalls seen in titles

- "Director" in agencies and banks is often a middle rank, not a function head.
- "Head of" in a 10-person team may be a one-person function.
- "Customer Success" is account management in B2B, not support.
- "Operations" alone spans warehouse to revenue ops — always qualify it.
- Recruiters for a function ("Talent Acquisition — Engineering") match the function's keywords.
