# Client materials: what to collect and what each feeds

Ask the client (as `bh question add` rows) for what is missing. A material that is not in the list
is still read, but it closes no gap.

| Material | Holds | Feeds brief part |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Website | The client's own site (not a competitor's, not a review of them), including the pages for suppliers and partners | About, Product, Who we sell to, What we say |
| Discovery call | Transcript or notes of the discovery / onboarding call — the brief cannot be written without it | About, Who we sell to, What we say |
| Agreement | What was sold and agreed: goals, target, deadlines | About (and the goal) |
| Sales deck | The pitch, the positioning, the proof | Product |
| Price list | Plans, prices, discounts, the model | Product |
| Cases | The client, the task, the result | Product, What we say |
| Competitors | Who the client says they are measured against, as links or a comparison — checked against our own research, not copied | Product, Market and competitors |
| Sales call | A recorded call with a prospect — where the objections are | Product, Who we sell to, What we say |
| ICP list | Target accounts, or the firmographics that describe them | Who we sell to |
| Won and lost | The client's CRM export — what an ICP is checked against | Who we sell to |
| Exclusions | Current clients, competitors, the do-not-contact list | Who we sell to → `bh dnc add`, `bh exclusion add` |
| Previous outreach | Every campaign they or an earlier agency ran, its replies, calls and lists — read first, checklist below | Previous outreach, Product (objections), What we say |

Market research is not a material: it is our own work, done with our own web tools and written
into part 5 of the brief with research markers.

## Previous outreach

The most useful material and the one least often asked for. An hour on one client's live project gave
the only objection anyone had recorded, the channel that worked, the real signature, four audiences
better than ours and 2,020 rows to dedupe against. Ask for it before writing the brief — an export,
read access to their sending tool, an earlier agency's report — and read it against this checklist:

- **Campaigns** — each one's name, channel, dates, audience and status; for a paused or stopped one,
  why.
- **Sends and replies per channel** — email sends and replies; LinkedIn invites, acceptances and
  replies; calls and connects. Keep the base of every rate: replies per send and replies per accepted
  invite are different numbers.
- **Every reply, read in full** — not the counts. Sort them into interest, objection, referral, not
  now, not me, unsubscribe; quote the objections.
- **Calls** — the disposition vocabulary their callers use, and how many calls ended in each.
- **Meetings** — every one, with where it came from: channel, list, persona, message. One meeting is
  still evidence about the channel.
- **Lists** — where the leads came from (a database, a directory, an event, a purchase), how many,
  and how many companies failed their own fit check.
- **Address statuses** — valid, catch-all, invalid, bounced, as a distribution.
- **Sending identity** — sender names, titles, domains, mailboxes, signatures.

What could not be obtained is written into the brief and asked for (`bh question add`). The people
already reached are a dedupe list before anyone is written to twice.

## Reading the website

Prefer, in this order, and spread across them rather than reading six product pages: what they sell
(products, services, solutions) → what it costs (pricing, plans) → who buys it (customers,
industries, case studies) → how they beat the alternative (comparisons, "why us", testimonials) →
who they are (about, team). Then the pages written for suppliers, partners and investors ("become a
vendor", "partner with us", investor relations): the business model is usually stated plainly there
and nowhere else. Skip single blog posts, news, job ads, login, legal and contact pages.

## Reading a deck or scanned page

Take every word as shown, keeping each value beside its label. Do not name a logo that has no
readable letters — that is how invented customers get into a brief.

## Won/lost and ICP lists

Use them to check the stated ICP: which industries, sizes and geographies the won deals actually
are. Where they disagree with what the client said on the call, it is a question, not a correction.
